# Case study and interview summary

## Claims manager summary

Northstar Mutual can use the 72-hour file to identify a small subset of claims worth checking for repair billing, documentation, duplicate payments or possible recovery. The app shows why a file was surfaced and how much discretionary review capacity it could consume. Staff must confirm evidence and protect ordinary claim service. The synthetic replay found that better ranking alone did not make the default additional-review policy financially viable.

## Technical contribution

The project demonstrates a reproducible relational ledger, as-of feature extraction, label-maturity-aware validation, conditional cost modeling and a capacity-constrained review optimizer. The Streamlit app consumes immutable predictions and analytics. The policy replay isolates ground truth from the scoring and queue path, then attributes prevention and recovery after selecting reviews.

## Resume and interview

Built a full-stack claims analytics case study using Python, DuckDB, scikit-learn, LightGBM, SciPy mixed-integer optimization and Streamlit. Generated 120,000 synthetic claims across four years, reconciled claims finances and reproduced six intervention policies across eight operational scenarios. Demonstrated how test-time model lift can coexist with negative simulated operational value.

Discuss the cost of rank-ordering errors, calibration plateaus, maturity exclusions, case-mix residuals, and effects of action-match assumptions. For production, first validate comparable cohorts and data lineage, estimate causal effects from a safe randomized or staged intervention pilot, monitor prompt settlement and disparate review burden, and define a rollback policy.
